**Scenario 2 (\#11 from de-escalation scenarios list)**

**Intro to the student**

The family medicine clinic is running behind schedule. You are told that your next patient is a 45-year-old man who has been waiting for over an hour. When you enter the room, he immediately says, "I waited all this time for a useless appointment." He explains that he was told today’s appointment was for laboratory testing, but clinic staff have now told him that the labs were supposed to be completed beforehand. He becomes increasingly frustrated, raises his voice, and states that the clinic has wasted his time.

Your goal is to reduce the patient’s frustration using appropriate de-escalation strategies, clarify the misunderstanding, and determine how the visit can still be useful.

**De-escalation bar**

Start: 5/10

Goal: 0/10

| Action Type | Description | Point Change | Scene Change |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Helpful Actions** |  |  |  |
| **Acknowledge frustration and apologize (KEY)** | Validate that the long wait and conflicting instructions were frustrating and offer a sincere apology | \-3 | Clinician turns away from the computer to face the patient, patient lowers his voice and says, "Exactly, I just don’t want my time wasted." |
| **Clarify the misunderstanding** | Ask what the patient was told and explain what appears to have happened without assigning blame to patient or staff | \-2 (with acknowledgment of pt frustration), \-1 (without) | Patient becomes less defensive and begins explaining what he expected from the appointment |
| **Collaborative problem-solving** | Discuss concrete options for what can still be accomplished today, including obtaining/ordering labs and arranging appropriate follow-up | \-2 (with acknowledgment of pt frustration), \-1 (without) | Patient shifts focus from the wait to discussing what can be done today |
| **Reset expectations** | Clearly explain the purpose of today’s visit, what can and cannot be accomplished, and what the next steps will be | \-2 (with acknowledgment of pt frustration), \-1 (without) | Patient appears calmer and begins asking questions about the plan |
| **Offer control** | Give reasonable choices about how to proceed, such as addressing other concerns today, arranging labs, or rescheduling if appropriate | \-1 | Clinician turns the computer screen toward the patient to show the lab order and scheduling options, patient leans in to look |
| **Calm verbal communication** | Maintain a calm, respectful tone and allow the patient to speak without interruption | \-1 | Clinician sits on the stool at eye level instead of standing, patient lowers his voice and conversation becomes more reciprocal |
| **Neutral Actions** |  |  |  |
| **Generic reassurance** | Offer reassurance such as "We’ll figure it out" without acknowledging the specific problem or providing a concrete plan | 0 | No meaningful change; patient remains frustrated |
| **Redirect to medical history** | Begin routine medical questioning without first addressing the patient’s frustration about the wait and miscommunication | 0 | Patient answers minimally and remains visibly irritated |
| **Unhelpful Actions** |  |  |  |
| **Defensive response** | Defend the clinic or justify the delay, such as "We’re very busy today" or "Everyone has been waiting" | \+3 | Patient raises his voice and responds, "That’s not my problem. I was told to come here." |
| **Blame the patient** | Tell the patient that he should have known or should have completed the labs before the appointment | \+3 | Patient becomes significantly more upset and insists that he followed the instructions he was given |
| **Blame other staff** | Criticize or blame the scheduler, front desk, or another staff member for the misunderstanding | \+2 | Patient remains frustrated and expresses decreased confidence in the clinic |
| **Dismiss/minimize concern** | Minimize the wait or frustration with statements such as "It’s only been an hour" or "There’s no reason to be this upset" | \+3 | Clinician keeps typing at the computer while the patient talks, patient becomes louder and states that the clinician is not listening |
| **Authoritative/confrontational tone** | Tell the patient to "calm down" or respond in a demanding or confrontational manner | \+3 | Patient becomes more defensive, raises his voice, and may threaten to leave |
| **No action** | Fail to acknowledge or respond to the patient’s escalating frustration for \>2 minutes | \+1 over time | Patient becomes increasingly frustrated and repeats that his time has been wasted |
| **Security/forced removal** | Call security or attempt to remove the patient solely for verbal frustration without threatening or dangerous behavior | END SIMULATION | END SIMULATION.
