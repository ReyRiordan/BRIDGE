**Scenario 3 (\#10 from de-escalation scenarios list)**

**Intro to the student**

You are on the internal medicine service. Your next patient is a 47-year-old man admitted overnight for evaluation of a suspected gastrointestinal bleed after presenting with melena and symptomatic anemia. He is awaiting repeat labs and a possible endoscopy later today. The nurse stops you outside the room and tells you the patient has become increasingly frustrated with the hospitalization, has started removing his monitoring leads, and says he needs to leave to get to work. When she explained that leaving could be dangerous, he raised his voice and told her that no one can keep him here. A tech and a second nurse are still in the room after trying to reattach his leads.

Your goal is to reduce the patient’s frustration using appropriate de-escalation strategies, ensure he understands the risks of leaving, and work toward a plan that protects his safety while respecting his autonomy.

**De-escalation bar**

Start: 6/10

Goal: 0/10

| Action Type | Description | Point Change | Scene Change |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Helpful Actions** |  |  |  |
| **Acknowledge autonomy and outside pressures (KEY)** | Recognize that he has responsibilities outside the hospital and state plainly that the decision is ultimately his | \-3 | Extra staff step out of the room, patient stops pulling at leads, lowers his voice, and says, "I’m not trying to be difficult, I just can’t lose this job" |
| **Explore the specific barrier** | Ask what is waiting for him at work, whether anyone can be called, and what he is most worried about missing | \-2 (with acknowledgment of autonomy), \-1 (without) | Nurse closes the door and draws the curtain for privacy, patient sits back down and begins describing his work situation |
| **Explain risks in plain language** | Describe what an active GI bleed can do, using concrete and non-threatening terms, and check his understanding | \-2 (with acknowledgment of autonomy), \-1 (without) | Patient goes quiet, asks a clarifying question about the bleeding |
| **Collaborative problem-solving** | Offer concrete options such as expediting labs, contacting the endoscopy team for a time estimate, or arranging a call to his employer | \-2 (with acknowledgment of autonomy), \-1 (without) | Patient shifts from leaving to asking how long the workup will actually take |
| **Give a concrete timeline** | Commit to a specific update time and follow through with what can and cannot be promised | \-2 | Patient agrees to wait for the update and stops asking for discharge paperwork |
| **Offer control** | Give reasonable choices such as which tests to prioritize, when to be updated, or whether to speak with the attending directly | \-1 | Patient engages, posture relaxes |
| **Calm verbal communication** | Sit down, maintain open posture, let him finish speaking without interruption | \-1 | Clinician pulls a chair to the bedside and sits at eye level, conversation becomes reciprocal, patient’s volume drops |
| **Neutral Actions** |  |  |  |
| **Generic reassurance** | Statements such as "You’re in good hands" or "We’ll take care of it" with no specific plan attached | 0 | No meaningful change; patient remains frustrated |
| **Redirect to medical history** | Begin routine questioning without addressing the stated desire to leave | 0 | Patient answers minimally, keeps glancing at the door |
| **Restate hospital policy** | Explain visiting rules, discharge procedures, or unit routines without addressing his concern | 0 | Patient interrupts and repeats that he needs to leave |
| **Unhelpful Actions** |  |  |  |
| **Tell the patient he cannot leave** | Statements such as "You’re not allowed to leave" or "I can’t let you go" | \+3 | Patient stands, raises his voice, and states that he is being held against his will |
| **Weaponize AMA paperwork** | Threaten that leaving AMA means his insurance will not cover the stay or that he will not be allowed back | \+3 | Patient becomes angrier and accuses the team of using threats to control him |
| **Dismiss or minimize concern** | Statements such as "Your job can wait" or "This is more important than work" | \+3 | Patient says the clinician does not understand his situation and asks for discharge papers |
| **Authoritative or confrontational tone** | Tell the patient to calm down, or respond in a demanding manner | \+3 | Clinician stays standing over the bed, patient becomes more defensive and moves toward the door |
| **Fear-based coercion** | Describe worst-case outcomes in graphic terms without offering any plan or timeline | \+2 | Patient dismisses the warning as an attempt to scare him and disengages |
| **Scold about the monitoring leads** | Focus on the removed leads or equipment rather than the underlying frustration | \+2 | Patient pulls remaining leads off entirely |
| **No action** | Fail to acknowledge or respond to the patient’s escalating frustration for \>2 minutes | \+1 over time | Patient begins gathering his belongings |
| **Security or physical prevention** | Call security, block the doorway, or attempt restraint on a patient with intact capacity who is verbally frustrated | END SIMULATION | END SIMULATION.
